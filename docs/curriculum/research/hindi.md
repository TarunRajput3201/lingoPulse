## 2. Writing System: Devanagari (देवनागरी) & Orthography
- **Script Typology**: Abugida (alphasyllabary). Each consonant symbol carries an inherent vowel *schwa* (/ə/), modified by vowel signs (*matras*, मात्रा).
- **Vowels (Swar, स्वर)**:
  - 11 Standard Vowels: अ (a), आ (ā), इ (i), ई (ī), उ (u), ऊ (ū), ऋ (ṛ), ए (e), ऐ (ai), ओ (o), औ (au).
  - Vowel Modifiers: *Anusvara* (ं - nasalization), *Chandrabindu* (ँ - vocalic nasalization), *Visarga* (ः - voiceless aspirate).
- **Consonants (Vyanjan, व्यंजन)**:
  - 33 Primary Consonants organized phonetically by place and manner of articulation (Velar, Palatal, Retroflex, Dental, Labial):
    - Distinct 4-way contrast: Unvoiced Unaspirated (क k), Unvoiced Aspirated (ख kh), Voiced Unaspirated (ग g), Voiced Aspirated (घ gh).
    - Retroflex vs. Dental contrast: ट, ठ, ड, ढ vs. त, थ, द, ध.
    - Flaps (*Nukta* consonants): ड़ (ṛ) and ढ़ (ṛh).
  - Conjunct Consonants (*Samyukt Vyanjan*): Combining half-letters to represent consonant clusters (e.g., क + य = क्य *kya*, न + म = न्म *nma*, क्ष *ksha*, त्र *tra*, ज्ञ *gya*).

## 3. Typology & Grammatical Architecture
- **Word Order**: Strict **SOV** (Subject-Object-Verb). Auxiliary verbs and verb complexes conclude the sentence.
- **Postpositions (instead of prepositions)**:
  - Grammatical relations are marked by postpositions following nouns/pronouns:
    - का / के / की (*kā/ke/kī*): Possessive ("of", agrees with possessed noun).
    - को (*ko*): Direct object / indirect object marker ("to").
    - से (*se*): Instrument ("with/by"), origin ("from"), comparison ("than").
    - में (*meṁ*): "in".
    - पर (*par*): "on/at".
    - के लिए (*ke lie*): "for".
- **The Oblique Case (*Tiryak Rūp*)**:
  - When a noun or pronoun is followed by any postposition, it transforms into the oblique case form (e.g. *laṛkā* boy -> *laṛke ko* to the boy; *laṛke* boys -> *laṛkoṁ ko* to the boys).
- **Two Grammatical Genders**: All nouns are either Masculine or Feminine (no neuter). Verbs agree in gender and number with the subject (or with the object in ergative past tense).
- **Split-Ergativity**:
  - In transitive past perfective tenses, the subject takes the postposition ने (*ne*), and the verb agrees with the *direct object*, not the subject (*Rām ne rotī khāī* = Ram ate bread [bread is fem., so verb is fem.]).
- **Three Tiers of Second-Person Address (Respect Levels)**:
  1. आप (*āp* - formal/honorific): Used for elders, strangers, professionals, guests. Verb takes plural respectful ending (-te haiṁ).
  2. तुम (*tum* - informal/familiar): Used for friends, siblings, peers. Verb takes -te ho.
  3. तू (*tū* - intimate/derogatory): Used only in intimate religious prayer or extreme informality; learners are taught to avoid.

## 4. Hindi Proficiency Progression Framework
| Level | Focus Areas |
| :---: | :--- |
| **A1** | Devanagari script, matras, greetings (*Namaste*), introductions, polite forms (*āp*), basic nominal sentences with *honā* (to be), numbers (1–20), ordering tea/food, family relations. |
| **A2** | Daily routines, Present Habitual tense (-tā hai), Continuous aspect (rahā hai), postpositions (*ko, se, meṁ, par*), Oblique case, past tense of *honā* (*thā/thī/the*), directional questions. |
| **B1** | Perfective past tense, ergative marker *ne*, modal verbs (*saknā* can, *cāhie* should/need), compound verbs (*lenā/denā/jānā* as aspectual vectors), travel and market negotiation. |
| **B2** | Subjunctive mood, passive constructions, complex relative-correlative clauses (*jo ... vo*, *jaisā ... vaisā*), formal vs colloquial registers, cultural literature excerpts. |
| **C1** | Advanced literary and administrative Hindi (Shuddh Hindi), formal debate, nuanced emotional registers, news editorials, classical idioms (*muhāvare*). |

## 5. Common Learner Pitfalls
1. **Aspiration Contrast**: Conflating unaspirated consonants with aspirated consonants (e.g. *kān* ear vs *khān* mine; *bāl* hair vs *bhāl* forehead).
2. **Retroflex vs Dental**: Pronouncing retroflex sounds (ट, ड) as English dentals, or failing to curl the tongue tip against the hard palate.
3. **Gender Agreement with Non-Living Nouns**: Forgetting that inanimate objects have arbitrary gender (e.g., *kitāb* book is feminine, *mez* table is feminine, *ghar* house is masculine, *pānī* water is masculine).
