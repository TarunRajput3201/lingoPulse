# Language Curriculum Research: English (en)

## 1. Research Sources & Institutional References
- **Council of Europe**: Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR: A1 to C2).
- **Cambridge Assessment English**: Cambridge English Scale, Key (KET), Preliminary (PET), First (FCE), Advanced (CAE).
- **British Council & IELTS**: Core language descriptors, pronunciation guidelines, international English benchmarks.
- **ETS (TOEFL / TOEIC)**: Academic and professional communicative competence standards.
- **Oxford English Corpus**: Frequency-based Lexical Syllabus (Oxford 3000 & 5000).

---

## 2. Writing System & Orthography
- **Script**: Latin alphabet (26 letters: 5 vowels, 21 consonants).
- **Orthographic Depth**: Deep/opaque orthography. Grapheme-to-phoneme correspondence is inconsistent due to historical sound shifts (Great Vowel Shift) and extensive loanword absorption (e.g., *rough, though, through, cough*).
- **Phonetics & Phonology**: 44 phonemes in standard Received Pronunciation (RP) and General American (GA) (approx. 20 vowels/diphthongs, 24 consonants). Crucial distinctions include voiced/unvoiced *th* (/θ/ vs /ð/), liquid distinction (/r/ vs /l/), and the neutral schwa (/ə/).

---

## 3. Typology & Grammatical Architecture
- **Word Order**: Strict **SVO** (Subject-Verb-Object). Inversion occurs in questions (*Are you...?*), negative adverbial fronting (*Rarely have I seen...*), and conditional inversion (*Had I known...*).
- **Morphology**: Analytic language with residual fusional traits. No grammatical gender for nouns/adjectives (natural gender reflected only in 3rd person pronouns: *he, she, it*).
- **Verbal System**:
  - Tenses: Present, Past, Future (modal *will/shall*).
  - Aspects: Simple, Continuous/Progressive (*be + -ing*), Perfect (*have + past participle*), Perfect Continuous.
  - Phrasal Verbs: Highly idiomatic particle verbs (*give up, turn down, look after, put off*) critical for A2–B2 proficiency.
  - Modals & Conditionals: Zero, First, Second, Third, and Mixed conditionals; modal verbs of deduction, obligation, permission.

---

## 4. Regional Variations
- **British vs. American**:
  - Vocabulary: *flat/apartment, lift/elevator, biscuit/cookie, lorry/truck, holiday/vacation*.
  - Spelling: *-our/-or (colour/color), -re/-er (theatre/theater), -ise/-ize (realise/realize)*.
  - Grammar: Present perfect preference in UK (*I have just eaten*) vs. simple past in US (*I just ate*).
- **Instructional Policy**: Present standard International English, explicitly noting US/UK variants without penalizing either.

---

## 5. Common Learner Pitfalls
1. **Third-Person Singular *-s***: Omission of *-s* in present simple (*He go* vs *He goes*).
2. **Article Usage**: Overuse or omission of *the/a/an* (especially for speakers of Slavic, Chinese, Japanese, or Korean where articles do not exist).
3. **Prepositional Precision**: *in, on, at* for time and space (*in the morning, on Monday, at 5 PM*).
4. **Aspectual Confusion**: Mixing Present Simple (habitual/facts) with Present Continuous (actions in progress).
