## 2. Writing System & Orthography
- **Script**: Latin alphabet (21 traditional letters: *j, k, w, x, y* used only in loanwords).
- **Phonetics & Phonology**:
  - Transparent/shallow orthography.
  - Vowel Purity: 7 vowel phonemes (/a, e, ɛ, i, o, ɔ, u/), pronounced clearly without reduction to schwa.
  - Hard vs. Soft Consonants: *c* and *g* are soft (/tʃ, dʒ/) before *e, i*, and hard (/k, ɡ/) before *a, o, u*. The letter *h* acts as a silent hardener (*chiamare* /kjaˈma.re/).
  - Double Consonants (*doppie*): Geminate consonants held for double duration (*fatto* vs *fato*, *anno* vs *ano*, *palla* vs *pala*), changing lexical meaning.
  - Pronunciation of *gli* (/ʎ/) and *gn* (/ɲ/).

---

## 3. Typology & Grammatical Architecture
- **Word Order**: Flexible **SVO** with universal pro-drop (subject pronouns omitted unless emphasizing contrast: *Parlo italiano* vs *Io parlo italiano*).
- **Two Grammatical Genders**: Masculine (*-o*, pl. *-i*) and Feminine (*-a*, pl. *-e*). Nouns ending in *-e* can be masculine or feminine (pl. *-i*).
- **Definite & Indefinite Article Paradigm**:
  - Masculine: *il/i*, *lo/gli* (before *s+consonant, z, gn, ps, pn*), *l'/gli* (before vowels).
  - Feminine: *la/le*, *l'/le*.
  - Indefinite: *un, uno, una, un'*.
- **Preposizioni Articolate**: Systematic fusion of simple prepositions (*di, a, da, in, su*) with definite articles (*in + il* = *nel*, *di + la* = *della*, *a + gli* = *agli*).
- **Verbal System**:
  - Three conjugation groups: *-are, -ere, -ire* (with *-isc-* subgroup).
  - Compound Past (*Passato Prossimo*): Auxiliary selection (*avere* vs *essere*) with strict participle agreement when using *essere* (*Lui è andato*, *Lei è andata*).
  - *Passato Prossimo* vs *Imperfetto*: Distinct completed action vs habitual/descriptive background.
  - Subjunctive Mood (*Congiuntivo*): Present, Past, Imperfect, Pluperfect for doubt, emotion, opinion (*Penso che sia bello*).
  - Pronoun Combinations: Direct (*lo, la, li, le*), Indirect (*mi, ti, gli, le, ci, vi*), Partitive *ne*, Locative *ci*.

---

## 4. Regional Variations & Cultural Conventions
- **Standard Italian**: Based on literary Tuscan with Roman phonology.
- **Northern vs. Southern Preferences**: Northern Italian predominantly uses *passato prossimo* even for distant events; Southern Italian retains greater conversational use of *passato remoto*.
- **Register Distinction**: Informal *tu* vs. Formal *Lei* (grammatical third-person singular feminine).

---

## 5. Common Learner Pitfalls
1. **Double Consonant Neglect**: Failing to elongate geminates alters meaning (*casa* house vs *cassa* checkout/box; *sete* thirst vs *sette* seven).
2. **Preposition Confusion (*a* vs *in*)**: *Vado in Italia* (country/region/large island) vs *Vado a Roma* (city/town).
3. **Pronominal Verbs**: Verbs with idiomatic attached clitics (*andarsene* = to leave, *farcela* = to manage to do something).
