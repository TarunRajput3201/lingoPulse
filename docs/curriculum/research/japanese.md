## 2. Writing System & Orthography
Japanese employs a tripartite writing system combining phonetic and logographic components:
1. **Hiragana (ひらがな, 46 base characters)**: Native grammatical inflections, particles, verb/adjective endings, and native words lacking common kanji.
2. **Katakana (カタカナ, 46 base characters)**: Foreign loanwords (*gairaigo* like コーヒー, ホテル), scientific/biological terms, onomatopoeia, and emphasis.
3. **Kanji (漢字, approx. 100 for N5, 300 for N4, 650 for N3, 1,000 for N2, 2,136 for N1)**: Ideographic characters borrowed from Chinese. Each kanji generally has:
   - *Onyomi* (音読み): Sino-Japanese phonetic reading, typically used in multi-kanji compounds (*jukugo*).
   - *Kunyomi* (訓読み): Native Japanese semantic reading, typically used for standalone kanji or stems with okurigana.
4. **Phonetics & Phonology**: Mora-timed language (rather than stress-timed). Features clean vowel system (/a, i, u, e, o/), vowel length distinction (*obaasan* grandmother vs. *obasan* aunt), geminate consonants (*sokuon* っ), and pitch accent (e.g. *hashi* bridge vs. chopsticks).

## 3. Typology & Grammatical Architecture
- **Word Order**: Strict **SOV** (Subject-Object-Verb). Verb always concludes the clause.
- **Topic-Prominent & Pro-Drop**: The particle は (*wa*) establishes conversational topic (*theme*), allowing known subjects or objects to be dropped naturally in authentic dialogue.
- **Particle System (*Joshi*, 助詞)**:
  - は (*wa*): Topic marker.
  - が (*ga*): Grammatical subject / focus marker.
  - を (*o/wo*): Direct object marker.
  - に (*ni*): Specific point in time, destination, indirect object, agent in passives.
  - で (*de*): Location of action, instrument, means of transit.
  - へ (*e*): Direction of motion.
  - と (*to*): Exhaustive listing ("and"), quotation, accompaniment ("with").
- **Verb Conjugations & Groups**:
  - Group 1 (Godan / consonant-stem verbs): e.g. *kaku, nomu, iku*.
  - Group 2 (Ichidan / vowel-stem verbs): e.g. *taberu, miru*.
  - Group 3 (Irregular verbs): *suru* (to do) and *kuru* (to come).
  - The Essential **Te-form (て形)**: Connects clauses, requests (*~te kudasai*), progressive actions (*~te iru*), permission (*~te mo ii*).
- **Honorific System (*Keigo*, 敬語)**:
  - Plain / Casual Form: Used with friends, family, peers.
  - Polite Form (*Teineigo*, です/ます): Standard polite register used with strangers, colleagues, service personnel.
  - Respectful (*Sonkeigo*): Elevates the listener/third party.
  - Humble (*Kenjōgo*): Lowers oneself/in-group actions.

| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **N5** | **A1** | ~100 | ~800 | Hiragana, Katakana, basic kanji, basic particles, polite verbs, daily routines, introductions. |
| **N4** | **A2** | ~300 | ~1,500 | Te-form combinations, potential/volitional forms, giving/receiving (*ageru/kureru/morau*), conditional *tara*. |
| **N3** | **B1** | ~650 | ~3,750 | Passive, causative, keigo basics, reading short essays, natural spoken contractions. |

| **N1** | **C1** | ~2,136 | ~10,000 | Academic prose, literary structures, full keigo mastery, specialized professional discourse. |

## 5. Common Learner Pitfalls
1. **Particle Confusion (*wa* vs. *ga*)**: *wa* highlights what follows it (the predicate); *ga* highlights what precedes it (the subject).
2. **Counters (*Josūshi*)**: Different counter suffixes based on physical shape (e.g., 本 *hon* for cylindrical objects, 枚 *mai* for flat objects, 匹 *hiki* for small animals).
3. **Overusing Personal Pronouns**: Overuse of *watashi* (I) or *anata* (you), which sounds unnatural or impolite in native Japanese.
