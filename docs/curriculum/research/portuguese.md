# Language Curriculum Research: Portuguese (pt)

## 1. Research Sources & Institutional References
- **Instituto Camões (Portugal)**: Quadro Europeu Comum de Referência para as Línguas (QECR) and CAPLE (Centro de Avaliação de Português Língua Estrangeira).
- **Ministério da Educação do Brasil (MEC)**: Certificado de Proficiência em Língua Portuguesa para Estrangeiros (CELPE-Bras).
- **Acordo Ortográfico da Língua Portuguesa (1990)**: Standardized cross-national orthographic reform.
- **Academia Brasileira de Letras (ABL) & Academia das Ciências de Lisboa**: Standard lexical and grammatical consensus.

---

## 2. Writing System & Orthography
- **Script**: Latin alphabet (26 letters + diacritics: acute *á, é, í, ó, ú*, circumflex *â, ê, ô*, tilde *ã, õ*, grave *à* for crasis, cedilla *ç*).
- **Phonetics & Phonology**:
  - Nasal Vowels and Diphthongs: Crucial phonemes marked with tilde or syllable-final *m/n* (/ɐ̃, ẽ, ĩ, õ, ũ/, diphthongs *-ão, -ões, -ãe*).
  - Open vs. Closed Vowels: Acute accent denotes open vowel (*avó* /aˈvɔ/ grandmother); circumflex denotes closed vowel (*avô* /aˈvo/ grandfather).
  - Brazilian Portuguese (BP) vs European Portuguese (EP) Phonology:
    - Palatalization in BP: *t* and *d* become [tʃ] and [dʒ] before /i/ sound (*noite, dia*).
    - Vowel Reduction in EP: Unstressed vowels are heavily reduced or elided in EP, creating a stress-timed cadence; BP maintains syllable-timed cadence with vocalic fullness.
    - Suffix *-l*: Vocalized to [w] in BP (*Brasil* -> [bɾaˈziw]); velarized /ɫ/ in EP.

---

## 3. Typology & Grammatical Architecture
- **Word Order**: Flexible **SVO** with pro-drop characteristics.
- **Two Grammatical Genders**: Masculine (*-o*) and Feminine (*-a*), with regular plural in *-s*.
- **Verbal Conjugation & Modes**:
  - Three conjugation paradigms: *-ar, -er, -ir*.
  - Essential Contrasts:
    - **Ser vs. Estar**: Permanent characteristic/identity vs. temporary state/location.
    - **Pretérito Perfeito vs. Pretérito Imperfeito**: Definite completed past action vs. ongoing/habitual past.
    - **Future Subjunctive (*Futuro do Subjuntivo*)**: Highly distinctive feature preserved in Portuguese (*Se eu tiver tempo...* = "If I have time...").
    - **Personal Infinitive (*Infinitivo Pessoal*)**: Rare Romance feature where the infinitive conjugates for person (*para nós fazermos* = "for us to do").

---

## 4. Regional Variations: Brazilian (BP) vs. European (EP)
- **Second-Person Address**:
  - BP: Predominantly uses *você* (conjugated with 3rd person singular) for informal and everyday address; *o senhor / a senhora* for formal respect.
  - EP: Strictly distinguishes informal *tu* (2nd person singular) from formal *você* or omitting the pronoun.
- **Pronoun Clitic Placement**:
  - BP: Prefers proclisis (placing object pronouns before the verb, e.g. *Me dá um café*).
  - EP: Enclisis is standard, with hyphenation (*Dá-me um café*).
- **Continuous Aspect**:
  - BP: *estar + gerund* (*Estou comendo*).
  - EP: *estar + a + infinitive* (*Estou a comer*).
- **Vocabulary Distinctions**:
  - *trem* (BP) vs. *comboio* (EP) - train.
  - *ônibus* (BP) vs. *autocarro* (EP) - bus.
  - *celular* (BP) vs. *telemóvel* (EP) - mobile phone.
  - *café da manhã* (BP) vs. *pequeno-almoço* (EP) - breakfast.

---

## 5. Common Learner Pitfalls
1. **Nasal Diphthongs**: Difficulty producing *-ão* (often mispronounced as plain -an or -on).
2. **False Friends with Spanish**: *propina* (tip in Spanish; bribe in BP); *exquisito* (delicious in Spanish; weird/strange in Portuguese).
3. **Crasis (*crase*, `à`)**: Contraction of preposition *a* with feminine article *a* (*Vou à praia*).
