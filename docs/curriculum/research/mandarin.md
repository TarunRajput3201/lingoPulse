## 2. Writing System & Orthography
- **Script Principle**: Morphosyllabic logographic writing system (*Hanzi*, 汉字). Characters represent both a syllable and a morpheme.
  - **Simplified Chinese (简体字)**: Official script of Mainland China and Singapore.
  - **Traditional Chinese (繁體字)**: Official script of Taiwan, Hong Kong, Macau, and historical diaspora.
  - **Stroke Order (笔顺)**: Top to bottom, left to right, horizontal before vertical, outside before inside, inside before closure.
  - **Radicals (部首, 214 Kangxi radicals)**: Provide semantic clues to meaning (e.g. 氵 water, 亻 person, 木 tree, 讠 speech).
- **Phonetics & Phonology (Hanyu Pinyin, 汉语拼音)**:
  - Official Latin transliteration system.
  - Syllable Structure: Initial + Final + Tone.
  - 21 Initials (b, p, m, f, d, t, n, l, g, k, h, j, q, x, zh, ch, sh, r, z, c, s).
  - 36 Finals (simple, compound, nasal).
  - **The Four Tones + Neutral Tone**:
    1. First Tone (High level, 55): 妈 (*mā* - mother).
    2. Second Tone (Rising, 35): 麻 (*má* - hemp).
    3. Third Tone (Dipping, 214): 马 (*mǎ* - horse).
    4. Fourth Tone (Falling, 51): 骂 (*mà* - to scold).
    5. Neutral Tone (Light/short): 吗 (*ma* - question particle).
  - **Tone Sandhi Rules**: Third-tone sandhi (when two 3rd tones appear consecutively, the first becomes a 2nd tone: 你好 *nǐ hǎo* -> [ní hǎo]); tone changes for 不 (*bù* becomes *bú* before 4th tone) and 一 (*yī*).

## 3. Typology & Grammatical Architecture
- **Word Order**: Primarily **SVO**, but frequently **Topic-Comment** structure where the topic is fronted (*那本书我已经看了* = "That book, I have already read").
- **Isolating / Analytic Typology**:
  - No grammatical gender, no grammatical case, no plural agreement (nouns do not change form).
  - Verbs do NOT conjugate for person, number, or tense.
- **Aspect Markers (instead of verbal tenses)**:
  - 了 (*le*): Perfective aspect indicating completed action or change of state.
  - 过 (*guo*): Experiential aspect indicating past experience ("have ever done").
  - 着 (*zhe*): Durative/progressive aspect indicating continuous state.
  - 正在 (*zhèngzài*): Dynamic ongoing action ("in the process of doing").
- **Measure Words / Classifiers (量词, *Liàngcí*)**:
  - Mandatory grammatical particles inserted between numbers/demonstratives and nouns:
    - 个 (*gè*): General classifier.
    - 本 (*běn*): Books/bound volumes (*两本书* = two books).
    - 张 (*zhāng*): Flat objects, paper, tickets, tables (*一张票* = one ticket).
    - 只 (*zhī*): Animals, birds, one of a pair (*一只猫* = a cat).
    - 杯 (*bēi*): Cups/glasses of liquid (*一杯咖啡* = a cup of coffee).
- **Structural Particles**:
  - 的 (*de*): Possessive / noun modifier (*我的书* = my book; *好喝的茶* = delicious tea).
  - 得 (*de*): Links verb to degree/manner complement (*跑得快* = runs quickly).
  - 地 (*de*): Adverbial marker modifying verbs (*慢慢地走* = walk slowly).
- **Sentence Particles**: 吗 (*ma* - yes/no question), 呢 (*ne* - bounce back question), 吧 (*ba* - suggestion/confirmation).

## 5. Common Learner Pitfalls
1. **Tone Inattention**: Pronouncing correct vowels and consonants with the wrong tone leads to complete miscomprehension (e.g. *wèn* to ask vs *wěn* to kiss).
2. **Missing Measure Words**: Saying "二书" instead of "两本书".
3. **Overusing "是" (*shì*) as a copula**: In Chinese, stative adjectives act as verbal predicates and do NOT take *shì* (e.g. *Tā hěn gāoxìng* = "He is happy", NOT *Tā shì gāoxìng*).
