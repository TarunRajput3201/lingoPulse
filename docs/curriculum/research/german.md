# Language Curriculum Research: German (de)

## 1. Research Sources & Institutional References
- **Goethe-Institut**: Goethe-Zertifikat A1 through C2 syllabus, *Profile Deutsch* (CEFR alignment).
- **TestDaF-Institut & DSH**: Test Deutsch als Fremdsprache standards for higher education.
- **Rat für deutsche Rechtschreibung**: Official German orthography rules (Amtliche Rechtschreibung).
- **Duden & Institut für Deutsche Sprache (IDS)**: Grammatisches Informationssystem (grammis) and standard usage corpora.

---

## 2. Writing System & Orthography
- **Script**: Latin alphabet (26 base letters + 3 Umlauts: *ä, ö, ü* and the ligature *ß* / Eszett).
- **Capitalization**: Universal capitalization of all nouns (*das Buch, der Hund, die Zeit*), regardless of sentence position.
- **Phonetics & Phonology**:
  - Glottal Stop (*Knacklaut* /ʔ/) before initial vowels (*der Apfel*).
  - Terminal Devoicing (*Auslautverhärtung*): Voiced stops/fricatives (/b, d, g, v/) are pronounced unvoiced at word endings (*Tag* /taːk/, *Hund* /hʊnt/).
  - Ich-Laut (/ç/) vs. Ach-Laut (/x/): Complementary allophones depending on preceding vowels (*ich, Mädchen* vs *Buch, Dach*).

---

## 3. Typology & Grammatical Architecture
- **Word Order Rules**:
  - **Verb-Second (V2) in Main Clauses**: The finite verb occupies strictly the second position (*Heute gehe ich in die Schule*).
  - **Verb-Final in Subordinate Clauses**: Conjunctions like *weil, dass, obwohl, wenn* send the conjugated verb to the very end (*... weil ich heute keine Zeit habe*).
  - **Sentence Bracket (*Satzklammer*)**: In compound tenses or separable verbs, prefixes and infinitives/participles are placed at the end (*Ich rufe dich morgen an*; *Ich habe das Buch gelesen*).
- **Three Grammatical Genders**: Masculine (*der*), Feminine (*die*), Neuter (*das*).
- **Four Grammatical Cases**:
  1. **Nominative**: Subject (*Der Mann schläft*).
  2. **Accusative**: Direct object, destination (*Ich sehe den Mann*).
  3. **Dative**: Indirect object, static location (*Ich helfe dem Mann*).
  4. **Genitive**: Possession, formal prepositions (*Das Auto des Mannes*).
- **Preposition Categorization**:
  - Pure Accusative (*durch, für, gegen, ohne, um*).
  - Pure Dative (*aus, bei, mit, nach, seit, von, zu*).
  - Two-Way Prepositions (*Wechselpräpositionen*: *an, auf, hinter, in, neben, über, unter, vor, zwischen*) — Dative for static location (*Wo?*), Accusative for directed movement (*Wohin?*).
- **Adjective Endings**: Weak, strong, and mixed declension paradigms depending on preceding determiners.

---

## 4. Regional Variations
- **DACH Region**:
  - Germany (Federal Standard).
  - Austria (Austrian German / ÖWB): *Grüß Gott*, *Jänner* (January), *Erdäpfel* (potatoes), *Marille* (apricot).
  - Switzerland (Swiss High German / Schweizer Hochdeutsch): Absence of *ß* (substituted by *ss*), vocabulary variations (*Velo* for bicycle, *Spital* for hospital).

---

## 5. Common Learner Pitfalls
1. **Gender Guessing**: Assuming natural gender equals grammatical gender (e.g. *das Mädchen* is neuter due to the diminutive suffix *-chen*).
2. **Separable Verb Prefix Placement**: Forgetting to split the prefix to the end of main clauses (*Ich stehe um 7 Uhr auf*).
3. **Konjunktiv II for Politeness**: Using *würde + infinitive* vs *hätte/wäre/könnte* for polite requests (*Könnten Sie mir bitte helfen?*).
