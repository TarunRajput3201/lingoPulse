## 2. Writing System: Hangul (한글) & Orthography
- **Script Principle**: Featural alphabet invented by King Sejong the Great in 1443. Letters mimic the shape of speech organs (mouth, tongue, teeth, throat).
- **Letter Inventory**:
  - 14 basic consonants: ㄱ, ㄴ, ㄷ, ㄹ, ㅁ, ㅂ, ㅅ, ㅇ, ㅈ, ㅊ, ㅋ, ㅌ, ㅍ, ㅎ.
  - 5 double (tense) consonants: ㄲ, ㄸ, ㅃ, ㅆ, ㅉ.
  - 10 basic vowels: ㅏ, ㅑ, ㅓ, ㅕ, ㅗ, ㅛ, ㅜ, ㅠ, ㅡ, ㅣ.
  - 11 complex vowels/diphthongs: ㅐ, ㅒ, ㅔ, ㅖ, ㅘ, ㅙ, ㅚ, ㅝ, ㅞ, ㅟ, ㅢ.
- **Syllable Blocks**: Letters are grouped into 2-to-4 letter syllabic blocks (Initial Consonant + Medial Vowel + optional Final Consonant *Batchim*), e.g. ㅎ + ㅏ + ㄴ = 한.
- **Batchim (받침) & Phonological Rules**:
  - 7 Representative Stop Sounds: Final consonants neutralize to [ㄱ, ㄴ, ㄷ, ㄹ, ㅁ, ㅂ, ㅇ] when unreleased.
  - Resyllabification (*Yeon-eum*): A final consonant shifts to the initial position of a following vowel-initial syllable (*한국어* -> [한구거] *han-gu-geo*).
  - Nasalization, Tensification, and Liquid Assimilation rules.

## 3. Typology & Grammatical Architecture
- **Word Order**: Strict **SOV** (Subject-Object-Verb). Verb/adjective always concludes the predicate.
- **Agglutinative Morphology**: Extensive postpositional particles (*Josa*, 조사) attached to nominals:
  - 은/는 (*eun/neun*): Topic marker / contrast.
  - 이/가 (*i/ga*): Subject marker / identifier.
  - 을/를 (*eul/reul*): Object marker.
  - 에 (*e*): Time, destination, static location of existence (*issda*).
  - 에서 (*eseo*): Location of dynamic action, origin ("from").
  - 과/와 (*gwa/wa*) or 하고 (*hago*): "and", "with".
- **Descriptive Verbs (Adjectives as Verbs)**: Korean adjectives conjugate like verbs (*yeppeuda* = to be pretty -> *yeppeoyo* = is pretty).
- **Speech Levels & Honorific System**:
  1. **Hasipsio-che (하십시오체 - Formal Polite)**: Verb ending ~ㅂ니다/습니다. Used in presentations, military, news, initial business greetings.
  2. **Haeyo-che (해요체 - Informal Polite)**: Verb ending ~아/어요. The workhorse of standard adult conversation.
  3. **Hae-che (해체 / Banmal - Informal Casual)**: Verb ending ~아/어. Used strictly with close friends, younger siblings, children.
  4. **Subject Honorific Marker**: Suffix -(으)시- inserted into verb stems to elevate elders or respected third parties (*haseyo* vs *haeyo*).
  5. **Honorific Vocabulary**: Specialized honorific terms for common nouns and verbs (e.g. *bap* -> *jinji* for meal, *meokda* -> *deusida* for to eat, *jip* -> *daek* for home).

## 4. TOPIK-to-CEFR Alignment Strategy
| TOPIK Level | Approximate CEFR | Focus Areas |
| :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **TOPIK 1** | **A1** | Hangul mastery, basic self-introductions, ordering food, purchasing items, numbers (Sino-Korean vs Native Korean), basic polite endings (~아/어요). |
| **TOPIK 2** | **A2** | Daily conversations, phone calls, asking directions, past tense (~았/었어요), future intention (~(으)ㄹ 거예요), basic conjunctions. |
| **TOPIK 3** | **B1** | Social relationships, public services, expressing opinions, indirect speech, compound connectors (~(으)면서, ~기 때문에). |
| **TOPIK 4** | **B2** | News articles, cultural topics, idiomatic expressions, passive/causative, written formal plain form (~ㄴ/는다). |
| **TOPIK 5–6** | **C1–C2** | Professional and academic Korean, research debates, high-level Sino-Korean vocabulary (*Hanja-eo*), specialized discourse. |

## 5. Common Learner Pitfalls
1. **Two Number Systems**:
   - Sino-Korean (일, 이, 삼, 사...): Used for phone numbers, dates, minutes, money, bus routes.
   - Native Korean (하나, 둘, 셋, 넷...): Used with counters for counting objects, people, hours (*du si* = 2 o'clock).
2. **Subject/Topic Confusion (은/는 vs 이/가)**: 은/는 introduces old information or highlights contrast; 이/가 introduces newly discovered or focal subjects.
3. **Misusing Banmal**: Using informal casual speech with strangers or older persons causes immediate social offense.
